
by Anonymous
Patrologia Latina Volume 


Sermon 1. [For Epiphany: the end of a sermon]

Let us keep this day with such deep reverence. This day is adorned by three miracles. Today Christ chose to be baptized by John. Today he made wine from water. Today a new star, never seen before, which signified Christ, the eternal light, led the Magi to the manger where Christ lay. So celebrate this day with the greatest devotion, so that Christ, whose praise you are honoring by these acts, may himself reward you.

Sermon 2. On the Purification.

Although Christ and his most holy mother were without sin, and were not subject to the laws to which we sinners are subject, nevertheless, to teach us humility, Christ willed to be carried into the temple with offerings and to be purified as though he were a sinner. And as he came to the temple, righteous Simeon met him and took him in his arms, because he had received an answer from the Holy Spirit that he would not see death until he had seen the Lord Christ born. For this reason holy Church established that, on this day, all of us should come to church with candles and with the greatest devotion, in honor of holy Mary and her Son, so that the true light, which is Christ, may be kindled in our hearts. For when Christ came to the temple, religious men and devout women met him with candles, so that they might receive the true light with visible light. Therefore we urge you to celebrate this day with your alms, your devout prayers, and every good work, and today to weep over your sins. May he himself grant this.

Sermon 3. In Lent.

Dearest brothers, although at every time one ought to work at caring for your souls, divine providence has nevertheless made special provision for human weakness with these forty days, so that whatever you have done wrong throughout the whole year, you may correct during them. These days stand, in a way, for the whole year. For Christ himself commanded us nothing that he did not first fulfill in his own person. He fasted for forty continuous days, and he hungered and thirsted just as we do. Therefore, dearest brothers, since throughout the whole year we live as we please, in food and drink and every kind of vanity, let us at least in these days live more modestly and more chastely. Let us humble ourselves before God with fasting and tears. Let us go to your priests in repentance and confession. Let us accuse ourselves before them and before the Lord. And let us become our own judges, if we wish to escape the judgment of God, so that we may obtain true forgiveness from the Lord and come with joy to the day of the Resurrection. Christ himself teaches us what we must do in order to deserve his grace. For he commanded us, saying: “Break your bread for the hungry,” and so on. And have peace and charity with your neighbor, and then you—

Sermon 4. [A sermon whose beginning is lost]

—“Lord,” will enter the kingdom of heaven, but only the one who does the will of my Father. The Lord warns us, urges us, and teaches us, both through himself and through his saints, that we should not love this life, which is full of every sorrow and hardship, as though we were going to possess it forever. He calls us from this misery to heavenly joys, from this pilgrimage to our heavenly homeland. Why do we delay in coming to our Father, who loves us more than everything he has created? He embraces us with all desire. He does not give us things that fail, but things that are eternal. He does not give the wretched honors of this world, but a kingdom, so that we may reign together with him. He calls after us, saying: “Come to me, all you who labor and are burdened, and I will refresh you; and with me you will find rest for your souls.” Therefore, dearest brothers, today call upon the Holy Spirit, who appeared over the Lord’s disciples on this very day, strengthened them, and taught them all truth. Ask him to teach you, to make faith, truth, and the love of God and neighbor sweet to you, and to guide you in body and soul. For we know most truly that whenever we regret having sinned, whenever we weep and groan over our sins and run to the Lord in true repentance, then the Holy Spirit takes possession of our heart. Therefore, all together, call with me today upon the almighty Lord: for the sake of his mother and all his saints, may he make us sharers in the grace of the Holy Spirit, may that grace remain upon us, and may he lead us into eternal life. Amen.

Sermon 5. On Saint John the Baptist.

Dearest brothers, you should know that whatever honor and reverence is shown to the saints is shown to our Lord himself, whom they served while they lived. Just as we are pleased when one of our servants is treated well in someone else’s house out of love for us, so the Lord is pleased if, out of love for him, we honor and venerate his saints. Therefore I urge you to celebrate this day with your prayers and with every work of charity as much as you can. When you stand here in church, you should commend yourselves deeply to God, body and soul. You should ask him to hold you in his hands, so that the devil may have no power over you. First, you should pray for the whole Church and for all the faithful departed. You should also pray for your enemies, that God may turn them toward peace. You should give your alms, and you should keep faith and truth with one another. This is the kind of feast that pleases God. Today you should ask Saint John to be our messenger before the almighty Lord, so that God may preserve you in this present life in such a way that you may gain eternal life. Dearest brothers, you should know who this John was whose feast we celebrate today. He is the John who baptized Christ. He was so holy that the people of his time thought he was the Christ. But Saint John answered them that he was not even worthy to untie the strap of Christ’s sandals. This John also foretold that Christ would come into the world and redeem the whole human race. He is the John about whom the Lord himself gives this testimony: among those born of women, no one greater—that is, no one holier—has arisen than John the Baptist. And although he was so holy, he nevertheless separated himself from everyone and lived in the desert. There he was fed with poor, wild food and wore rough clothing made from camel’s hair. So if such a holy man, who had almost never sinned at all, afflicted himself in this way for love, what will become of us sinners, who sin every day, and yet every day live in great pleasures, eat to excess, and drink to drunkenness?

Sermon 6. Of Saint Peter the Apostle.

Dearest brothers, you should constantly think about how much labor the saints of God endured to gain the kingdom of heaven. They did not spare earthly possessions. They did not spare their bodies, but gave them for the sake of their souls. They endured every torture. Every punishment seemed small to them because of the love of God. They were bound, imprisoned, killed by the sword, and hung on the cross. So too Saint Peter, whose feast we celebrate today, was hung on a cross because he preached the word of God and loved the truth. For that reason he earned the privilege of receiving authority from the Lord: if he opened the gate of heaven for anyone who asked worthily, it would be opened for that person; and if he closed it to anyone, it would be closed. Therefore let us all together call upon Saint Peter today, whom the Lord established as prince of the apostles. Let us ask him to obtain from the Lord forgiveness for our sins and to open the gate of heaven. And just as Peter handed over his body for the sake of his soul, so we too must put to death in ourselves all our vices: greed, lust, pride, faithlessness, gluttony, and drunkenness. By enduring these sufferings and this struggle, may we deserve eternal life. Today also remind Saint Peter to remember the mercy with which the Lord had mercy on him when Peter denied him three times. For the Lord allowed Saint Peter to fall so that he could have compassion on us when we fall, because the Lord entrusted us to him as sheep to a shepherd, saying: “If you love me, feed my sheep; and just as I have forgiven you, so you also forgive them. You fell through weakness; each of them also often falls through weakness.” This authority of Peter is held by those who are called bishops, and by us priests as well, even though we are sinners. So whoever flees to us in true repentance, we forgive that person’s sins. Christ forgives; we are heralds and judges declaring that the sins have been forgiven. We assign penance to the penitent, and in this way we open heaven. Today ask Peter also to remember the holy Church entrusted to him, and our pope, so that almighty God may make him worthy to govern his Church. Then ask Peter to remember our secular judge, the emperor, and all rulers, so that almighty God may make them such people that, while they judge earthly matters justly, they may receive a heavenly reward. Then ask him for all the faithful departed, that today he may open the heavenly homeland to them. Ask him for yourselves as well: since he has authority to close and open heaven to whomever he wills, may he loosen the chains of your sins and open for us the gate of heaven. May he himself grant this.

Sermon 7. On the Assumption of Saint Mary.

All of you who have gathered today in honor of Saint Mary, the Mother of the Lord, call on her with your whole heart, so that she may intercede for your sins. Through her, the Lord has worked salvation for the human race. She is the star of the sea and the light of the whole world.

This world is like a sea, because it is always being tossed about by great instability and never remains in the same condition. Just as, through Eve and the devil’s persuasion, the human race fell, so through Mary, when the angel announced the message, salvation shone over the whole world.

She is the unique hope of all who flee to her for help. Through her, our Lord—her Son—forgives sins. Today, among the choirs of angels, in the sight of God, she rejoices with eternal joy. She gave birth for us to the eternal Light, Christ himself, the Son of God, through whom the world has been enlightened. She is the one to whom we sinners ought to run. She is our great hope.

So today lift up your hearts to the almighty Lord. Because of his Mother, who today was received into heaven in body and soul, may he look kindly on us and grant us faith, truth, true repentance, and eternal life.

And I also urge you: stand in church with the greatest devotion. Pray for what you need. Weep over your sins. Ask for time to amend your lives, so that by repentance you may wash away your sins. May he himself grant this.

Sermon 8. [The close of a sermon]

Now I commend your body and soul to almighty God, to Saint Mary his Mother, and to all his saints. May you live worthily here on earth, so that you may become sharers in eternal blessedness.

Sermon 9. On the Nativity of Saint Mary.

Dearest brothers, you have gathered today in honor of God and of his holy Mother. So stand in church in such a way that your presence here does not become a cause of condemnation for you.

Let each person look into his own heart and see what sins accuse him. Let him rebuke himself. Let him place his sins before his own eyes. Let him become the judge of his own sins, and let him punish himself with true repentance, because repentance is a penalty. Whoever does this will be safe on the day of judgment, because he has already gone ahead and judged himself. As Scripture says, “If you judged yourselves, you would certainly not be judged.”

Think carefully about your unhappy condition. This life must end in sorrow and great bitterness, and everything in this world must be left behind. The Lord does not look at riches. He does not look at noble birth. He does not look at beauty. All these things are brought to the same end. But in heaven, the person who was more righteous here becomes more noble, richer, and more beautiful.

For the wicked, the end of this life is the beginning of eternal fire. For the good, it is the beginning of eternal joy. The wicked are condemned with the devil, whom they have always served and whose will they have done. But the good rejoice with the heavenly King in unspeakable joy, and they reign with the angels and all the saints forever and ever.

So today ask the Mother of the Lord to help you. Appeal to her loving mercy, so that today she may fall at the feet of our Lord and beg him to rescue us from eternal punishments and grant us eternal joys.

Therefore I urge you: whoever among you can do so should feed a poor person today. Whoever cannot do that should give at least one act of charity. And whoever lacks even that should at least have a good will. May he himself grant this.

Sermon 10. On Saint Michael.

The Lord says in the Gospel: “Unless you turn and become like little children, you will not enter the kingdom of heaven.”

The way of little children is to be simple, without envy and without hatred. They do not return evil for evil. And if someone hurts them, they quickly forget it, and within the hour they smile again at the person who hurt them.

Whoever imitates such children, no matter how old they may be, are called children and sons of God. The holy angels guard people like that day and night, and they do not give the evil angels room to overcome them.

When we do something good, when we love what is right, when we live chastely and purely, the good angels prevail. But when we carry hatred and envy against our neighbor, when we desire what belongs to our neighbor, when we unjustly take from others what is theirs, then the devil prevails with his angels. That is why Michael is said to fight against the devil.

So, dearest brothers, at all times we ought to call on the protection of Saint Michael and all the holy angels, so that they may defend us from the devil’s traps and guard our souls, lest the devil snatch them away.

Protect yourselves against the devil’s malicious tricks. He never sleeps, but stays awake. As a wolf prowls around a flock, lying in wait to see whether he can seize some little sheep, so the devil prowls around, trying to seize our souls.

Therefore, dearest brothers, celebrate this feast of Saint Michael with full devotion. Give alms in his honor. Be kind to one another. Do not drive the holy angels away from guarding you. Today ask Saint Michael, who has authority over all souls, to present your souls in paradise when they depart from this body. May he himself grant this.

Sermon 11. On the Feast of All Saints.

Dearest brothers, every day the Lord urges us to turn back to him and do his will. And he promises us a reward: the kingdom of heaven. Every day he calls after us. He calls us his children. He invites us to the joys of heaven.

But we turn our ears away. We love this world more—this world where we possess nothing secure, nothing truly our own—than we love reaching that place where we will be fellow citizens with the angels and all the saints.

Brothers, you see how God has rewarded his chosen ones, whose feast we celebrate today. What joy, what delight, God’s chosen ones have in heaven—human speech cannot explain it, and no human heart can fully grasp it. Blessed are those who exchange a few short days for eternal gladness.

Who are they? They are the ones of whom the Lord himself says in the Gospel: “Blessed are the merciful.” These are the people who help orphans, widows, and all who are in hardship and misery, as much as they can. People like this will obtain mercy from the Lord.

And again the Lord says: “Blessed are the peacemakers.” These are the people who love peace, who harm no one, who defend the suffering, who are content with what is their own, who do not seize what belongs to others, and who are not tortured by hatred and envy. People like this will obtain eternal peace.

And again the Lord says: “Blessed are the humble,” those who endure many injuries for God’s sake. And again: “Blessed are the pure in heart,” those who plan no wickedness, who plot no evil, who are clean from murder, perjury, adultery, fornication, and greed. These are called children of God. Their reward will be abundant in heaven.

But truly miserable are those who, for the sake of these few short days, earn eternal punishment in eternal fire. Who are they? They are the proud, murderers, robbers, perjurers, the faithless, adulterers, fornicators, and drunkards. They will be with the devil in eternal fire.

So stand today before God and all his saints. Look into your heart, and see by what sins you are bound. Admit your guilt before God and his saints. Confess your sins to him, so that he may mercifully rescue you from them and lead you to the joys of heaven. May he himself grant this.

Sermon 12. Of Saint Martin.

Dearest brothers, holy Church does not celebrate the feasts of the saints each year without reason. It has been arranged for our salvation that we should flee to their protection and imitate their holy way of life as much as we can.

So, dearest brothers, imitate the works of Saint Martin, whose feast we celebrate today. See how great his mercy and goodness were, and how much he loved God. In the middle of winter, when the cold was extremely harsh, he had already given all his clothes to the poor except one cloak. Then he divided even that cloak in two: one part he gave to a poor man, and the other he kept for himself.

Because of this, that same night he saw Christ clothed in the half-garment with which he had clothed the poor man. Christ was saying to the holy angels, “Martin, still only a catechumen,” and so on.

Therefore, whatever is given to the poor is given to God. As Christ himself testifies: “What you did for one of the least of mine, you did for me.” And because of this great goodness, Martin deserved to receive from God the gift of even raising three dead people to life.

So today let us pray together that, through Martin’s intercession, we may be raised from eternal death. We also warn you, as we rightly ought, to obey God’s commandments and not despise his fatherly warnings, because he is always working for this purpose: to preserve us as his children for the eternal inheritance.

Therefore, dearest brothers, let us do works that never fail. What are those works? Let us keep faith and truth with all our neighbors. Let us do injury to no one. We ought to help everyone we can. Let us invite the poor into our homes. Let us give them clothing, food, and drink.

Since the Lord is among you every day in the form of a poor person, we ought to receive him often, so that he may also receive us—he with whom blessed Martin reigns without end. So call on Saint Martin, that he may be our intercessor, so that we may deserve the grace of God and rejoice with him in eternal joy. May he himself grant this.

Sermon 13. For any feast.

Dearest brothers, we urge you to love God with your whole mind and your whole heart. Entrust your body and your soul to him, and wherever you can, do his will, because without his grace you can do nothing.

Place yourselves in his hands. Keep him before your eyes. Ask him to soften your heart, so that you may be able to weep over your sins, because no one can help us unless he helps us.

Let no one put hope in wealth, or in treasure, or in great castles, or in broad estates. None of these things helps the person who has offended God. God takes them all away whenever he wills. You yourselves have often seen powerful rulers humbled, taken captive, and thrown into prison. You have seen many rich people reduced to extreme poverty. When we offend God, then nowhere should we think ourselves safe.

In many ways the Lord shows us that everything in this world is uncertain and passing away. So we ask you and warn you: for the sake of your soul, turn back to your God. Do not let this world deceive you, for it is full of toil and sorrow.

And yet now, among rich and poor alike, hardly anyone in this world aims at anything except becoming richer and piling up possessions—though he does not know to whom he will leave them, or when he will have to leave them behind.

So, in the love of almighty God, we urge you: keep in your heart the words we have spoken to you today, so that they may take root there and bear good fruit. Today I entrust you to the grace of the Holy Spirit. May he put good will and good works into your heart. May he himself grant this.

Sermon 14. Likewise, for any feast.

Whether we want to or not, we must pass out of this world. Blessed, then, are those who send some good ahead of them, so that they may live forever.

It is strange, dearest brothers: we worry about how we will live here for a few years, but we do not think about how we ought to live forever. Truly I tell you, nothing will be found there unless it has been sent ahead from here.

So press on, dearest brothers, toward your homeland. The Lord himself invites you and urges you to leave this pilgrimage behind. Do not be lazy. Greater things are found there than anyone could possibly send ahead. There is no labor there, no sorrow. There is nothing there but joy and gladness in the presence of God. There is rejoicing with the angels and with all his saints.

So pray to the one who loves us above every creature of his. Ask him to stretch out his hand to us and rescue us from the miseries of this age. May he himself grant this.

Sermon 15. Another sermon.

Dearest brothers, the Apostle says, “We have all sinned and lack the glory of God.” We are all sinners, but God’s mercy is greater than our sins. Let no one be terrified by his sins, however great they may be. Let him only turn back, do penance, and he will be saved.

For Scripture says, “You have sinned; now stop.” God does not reject sinners. He rejects those who refuse to repent.

If someone is an adulterer, or sexually immoral, or a murderer, or perjured, or a usurer, or caught in other crimes, he should not despair. The Lord came into this world for such people, to save them. As he himself says, “I did not come to call the righteous, but sinners.”

Look at the examples he has set before us. The thief on the cross, when he was about to die, repented of what he had done and said, “Lord, have mercy on me when you come into your kingdom.” And the Lord answered him, “Truly I tell you, today you will be with me in paradise.” In this way the Lord gave great hope to us, to thieves, sinners, and other wrongdoers: after true repentance, the thief entered glory before any of the saints.

David was guilty of adultery and murder. He repented of what he had done, and the Lord’s prophet Nathan said to him, “Your sin has been forgiven.” In this way the Lord gave great hope to all who are guilty of adultery and murder.

Mary was a sinful woman, full of every vice. She came with true repentance and many tears to the Lord’s feet and kissed them, until he forgave her all her sins. He honored her so greatly that after his resurrection he appeared first to her, and she announced the resurrection to the apostles. In this way he gave great hope to all men and women who have fallen because of human weakness.

Zacchaeus the tax collector had cheated many people. But through true repentance he restored everything fourfold, and so he was found worthy to receive Christ into his house. In this way the Lord gave great hope to merchants who buy or sell fraudulently.

True repentance is the real remedy against every sin. Matthew the tax collector had unjustly extorted goods from many people, but through true repentance he became an apostle and an evangelist. Peter denied the Lord, but through true repentance and tears he deserved to become the leader of the apostles.

All these things give great hope to us sinners. And I tell you: there is joy among God’s angels over the conversion of one sinner. Therefore let no sinner despair. On whatever day he turns back to the Lord, even if he has fallen into many crimes, he will be received with joy. The Lord is always calling after us to return to him.

Whoever is truly repentant may be confident about his sins being forgiven. But if any of you does not yet have true repentance, let him ask God and knock at the door of divine mercy. It will be opened to him, and he will be given what he asks.

So we ask you and warn you: keep God before your eyes at all times. He has given us everything we have, and he still promises us the kingdom of heaven. May the one who lives and reigns without end lead you there.

Sermon 16. Another.

Dearest brothers, the last and dangerous times are pressing in. Faith, truth, and all justice have failed. Faithfulness is hardly found anymore between servant and master, or between husband and wife. Every kind of injustice and emptiness has now grown so strong that God’s anger and his scourge rightly come upon us.

We see famine before you. We see a failure of grain and wine. We see plundering and fires throughout the whole land. We see nation rising against nation. We see the Roman Empire being destroyed. And beyond that, human laws have utterly failed. The world is already threatening to collapse.

So let us flee, so that we are not buried under the world when it falls. If someone were staying in a house that was shaking from age, would he not leave it at once and flee to safer places? Let us flee from this world and from this exile to our homeland in heaven. Our Father himself, who gave us body and soul, invites us there when he says, “Come, blessed ones of my Father.”

Therefore, as part of my office, I warn you and urge you: let each person question his own conscience, asking by which sins he has offended the Lord. Let him confess to God and to his priest. Let him judge himself, if he wishes to avoid God’s future judgment. As the Apostle says, “If you judged yourselves, you would certainly not be judged.”

Pay attention, dearest brothers. Life is short and uncertain for us. Just as a flower blooms today and tomorrow dries up and falls, so our life fades away.

Therefore keep watch at all times with almsgiving, fasting, prayer, and every good work. You do not know the day when the day of the Lord will come like a thief in the night and separate body from soul. After that, there will be no place left for repentance. Then we will go where we have deserved to go: either to eternal rest or to eternal fire.

Prepare a place for yourselves in heaven, where there is peace and gladness without end, and a joy like the one Scripture describes: “No eye has seen, nor has it entered into the human heart, what God has prepared for those who love him.” May the one who lives and reigns without end lead you to that joy.

Sermon 17. Another.

Dearest brothers, I warn you and urge you: take thought for your soul. It will remain forever, either in heaven or in hell.

So do every good work while you have time, because the whole span of this life has been given to us as a time for repentance. After death, every act of repentance will be fruitless.

Therefore prepare yourselves for the day of death. Clothe yourselves with humility, chastity, self-control, and love. These are the white garments that are pleasing in the sight of the highest King.

Strive to leave this place. Strive to come out from the midst of injustice, from the darkness of this world, into the promised land. There the choir of angels awaits us. There is the company of prophets. There all the saints come to meet us. There is our mother. There all our family rejoices.

When any one of us is about to go to court, or to some other place where many people gather, does he not dress himself in brighter clothing and arrange his hair, so that there will be nothing to offend the eyes of the crowd? How much more, when we are about to go into the presence of our Lord, should we be adorned with good works.

May he be pleased to grant this to you, he who with the Father and the Holy Spirit lives and reigns.

Sermon 18. Another.

You who are poor, you who lack the possessions of this world: God will comfort you, if you put your hope in him and are willing to bear your poverty with patience. Place all your hope in him, because he cares for you, and with him are riches that remain forever.

Christ did not promise the riches of this world to his chosen ones, but labor and sorrow in this present age. As he himself says, “The world will rejoice, but you will grieve; yet your grief will be turned into joy.” And when you reign with me, you will rejoice in eternal gladness.

So we urge you: carry your riches into heaven. There no thief breaks in, no robber takes them away, and no one more powerful can extort them from you. And just as you are poor in possessions, be poor also in desire. Seek nothing else, desire nothing else, except what God, by his grace, has offered you. And know this: those who fear God will lack nothing.

Many people possess the riches of this world to the condemnation of their own souls. Because of those riches they become proud, despise those who are poorer, plunder the poor, show no reverence to God, and do everything according to their own pleasure.

But the poor, who suffer hunger, thirst, and cold, more often flee for refuge to God. He restrains them through the hardships of this world, so that this world may not become sweet to them, and so that, having nothing in this world to delight them, they may more easily be drawn to him.

Therefore turn to him with your whole heart, and he will be the comfort of your poverty. He lives and reigns forever.

Sermon 19. Another.

It is necessary, dearest brothers, that we admonish you often, because we are your priests, and we will have to give an account for your souls if the devil, that invisible wolf, snatches any one of them away. Just as a shepherd must answer for a lost sheep, so a priest must answer for a lost soul.

Therefore we admonish you and beg you: preserve faith and truth among yourselves. Let no one defraud another. What you do not want done to yourself, do not do to someone else.

Love God, who loves you. Love your Father, who gave you soul and body. Keep him in your heart. Consider what his will is, and do that. His whole will and purpose is this: to preserve you.

Oh, what great dignity it is that he calls us his children! Truly, only those are blessed who love God, cling to him, call upon his name, and do good works.

Look: to make clear the love he had for us, he gave himself for our sins. We sinned, and he paid the debt for our sins.

Sermon 20. Sermon in the week that is called the Common Week.

We ought to explain to you why the Church has been accustomed to celebrate this week, more than other weeks of the year, with a greater practice of almsgiving.

This was established by the holy people who came before us, and by our ancestors, holy men who loved God with their whole heart. It seemed good to them that, after the gathering of the harvest, we should share some of it with the poor, in memory of the living and the dead and of our ancestors, and for the praise and glory of God, who bestowed these things on us.

For this reason it is commonly called the Common Week: so that everyone may celebrate this week together with every kind of good work, each person according to his means. In this way God may preserve and protect what he has given from fire, from robbers, and from every harmful attack.

Those to whom God has given the goods of this world should share with those who do not have them. For from the person to whom God has given more, he also requires more.

Let the poorer people pray to God for the rich, so that through their riches the rich may deserve eternal life. And let the poor comfort one another with the hope of eternal riches.

But the rich should not put their hope in the riches of this world, which pass away in a moment, together with the people who possess them. Instead, they should put their hope in eternal riches. There we will find the true and eternal treasure: Christ, the Son of God, our Lord, who gave us life and possessions.

To that same Lord I entrust your body and soul. Amen.

Sermon 21. [Admonition to priests (note in the margin)]

The apostle cries out, “The name of God is blasphemed because of you.”

We priests ought to preach to the people of God by words and by example. We ought to teach them the justice of God. We ought to guide the unlearned as sighted people guide the blind. But alas, we ourselves are blind to all justice and all truth. And both groups together, we who lead and they who follow, fall into the ditch.

What is this ditch? It is the power of the devil.

For this reason blessed Gregory says, “Bad priests are the ruin of the people.” And so the prophet cries out, “Woe to the priests who feed themselves with outward goods, while neglecting the people of God in spiritual goods.” And again the prophet cries, “Their blood, that is, their sins, I will require from your hands.”

Truly, the name of God is blasphemed because of you. For the very things we forbid others to do, we do ourselves. We preach chastity, and we ourselves are not chaste. We preach self-control, and we ourselves are uncontrolled. We preach fasting, and we ourselves do not fast. We forbid greed, and we ourselves are greedy. Everything we condemn in others, we ourselves commit.

The apostle himself trembles at this when he says, “May it not happen that, after preaching to others, I myself should become rejected.”

People provide us with outward goods and feed our bodies. We ought to feed their souls with spiritual food: that is, with the preaching of divine justice and truth.

We seek our own glory, not the glory of God. We boast in our power and in our riches, and we do not consider what has been entrusted to us. We consume the goods of the people, but we do not labor to win their souls for God.

I tell you the truth: God will require from us all those souls that perish through our negligence.

We do not govern the people of God with charity or gentleness. Instead, with a kind of harshness and resentment, we provoke them all the more to anger and complaint against us. Rather, we ought to encourage them with gentle admonitions, and draw them in with every kind of charity, so that we ourselves, together with them, may be preserved for eternal life.

Then the Lord could say to us, “Good and faithful servant, because you have been faithful over a few things,” that is, over these earthly things, “I will set you over many things,” that is, I will give you eternal life.

The bishop does not say to us, “Receive riches and use them however you want.” Instead, he says to us, “Receive the care of souls, for which you will have to give an account on the day of judgment.” This is something we must fear greatly. This is truly terrifying for us.

There is no calling on earth that could serve God more pleasingly than ours as priests, because we could save both ourselves and others. After all, any one of us loves more the servant who brings ten, or twenty, or even more sheaves into his barn than the servant who brings only one—that is, the one who saves only himself.

Therefore the Lord says to Peter, “If you love me, feed my sheep”—that is, feed them with spiritual pastures.

We ought to be intercessors for human beings before almighty God. We ought to be mediators between God and humanity. But how can we intercede for them, when we ourselves have offended God and lack his grace?

Sermon 22. On the Dedication.

Although God is present in every place where his name is invoked, he has nevertheless chosen a house for himself in a special way: a place where everyone may gather, where sacrifice may be offered to him, and where he has appointed his guardian angels day and night to present our prayers before him. It is a place where all may call upon him, and where the catholic faith may be preached, so that there may be for all one faith, one baptism, and so that all may be of one mind in Christ.

Therefore, dearest brothers, you who have gathered today in honor of our Lord Jesus Christ and of this holy dedication: prepare another temple for God, of which this stone building is the figure. Prepare a worthy house for God in your heart. Cleanse it from all hatred, fornication, adultery, perjury, greed, gluttony, and drunkenness. From the person whose heart is possessed by these and similar sins, God flees.

And after you have cleared out this filth, adorn that temple with humility, chastity, self-control, vigils, fasting, almsgiving, prayers, and the other good works. Light there the lamp of holy confession and true repentance. Bathe it with tears. Kindle there the fragrance of holy charity toward God and toward your neighbor.

Then God will dwell in your heart and make his home with you. You will be a true temple for God. As the apostle says, “The temple of God is holy, and that temple is you.”

In this way, as we said before, Zacchaeus the tax collector—about whom today’s Gospel reading spoke—earned the grace that God deigned to remain in his house and forgave him all his sins, because Zacchaeus prepared for him a place of true repentance.

So then, dearest brothers, invite God into the house of your heart, and offer him the sacrifice of a good will, so that after this life he may receive you into the eternal dwellings. May he himself grant this.

Sermon 23. On the day of a funeral.

It is written in the sacred books that the day of death is better than the day of birth, and that it is better to go to a house of mourning than to a house of feasting. The one who wrote this for our instruction knew well the misery and toil of this life.

As soon as we are born, we begin to cry. That is our first voice. And so, as long as we live, we are always amid labor and troubles. Look at kings and rulers, counts and other wealthy people. See how they struggle, and what anxieties they endure night and day. The rich, just like the poor, are taken captive, killed, and made to suffer many hardships. Until the day of their death they are never truly at peace.

But on the day of their death they are released from all the evils of this world. Then eternal rest and eternal joy begin for all who have feared God in this world and done his will. Then every misery ends. Then hunger and thirst end. Then every labor ends. There, where there is no plague and no enemy, there is the choir of angels. There we shall see the Son of God at the right hand of the Father. There we shall reign with Christ forever.
Dearest brothers, you should also know why it is better for you to go to the house where we see the dead, where there is groaning and mourning, than to go to feasts where there is gladness. When we see the dead, we immediately think about what will happen to us. We think about the end to which our life, our possessions, and all worldly glory are coming.

We are reminded that we must leave everything we have here, and that as we entered naked, so we shall leave naked. Only then will we realize that we do not know for whom we gathered all these things. Soon we begin to decay, and others begin to use our goods. Nothing from all our possessions ever remains with us, unless perhaps we sent something ahead beforehand. Only our good works and our good will remain with us.
And you should know this for certain: at the passing of each one of us, the angels of God and the angels of the devil will be present, and both sides will claim what belongs to them. If the good works are greater, the angels of God will prevail and carry the soul away into paradise, before the face of God. But if the evil works are more numerous, then the messengers of the devil will prevail and lead the soul before the devil, so that it may burn without end in eternal fire.

Therefore I beg you, for the sake of almighty God, pay attention to the danger that lies ahead of you. Remember your last day. Prepare yourselves for that place where Christ himself, with his angels, is waiting for you and inviting you.

Today pray to almighty God that, through the intercession of holy Mary his mother and of all his angels, he may rescue the soul of this good man from the power of the devil, and preserve it for eternal rest and eternal joy, which will never fail.

Sermon 24. On Sunday.

We must honor every Lord’s Day with every effort toward reverence and holiness, because the Lord’s Day is the first day, the day on which Christ began to make the world. On that day the angels were created. On that day manna first came to the people of Israel. On that day Christ rose from the dead. On that day the Holy Spirit was given to the apostles. And that same day will be the last day of this world, the day on which we too will rise again, and on which each person will receive a reward according to his works.

For this reason it pleased the apostles that the Lord’s Day should be kept free from every servile work. And it is called the Lord’s Day because on that day we are not allowed to do anything that displeases our Lord.

Therefore, since today we must devote ourselves to the works that please God, it seemed fitting to speak to you about the dignity and holiness of this day. When you have heard this, may you embrace God with both fear and love, may you thus comfort your souls; and on this day we ought to devote ourselves to good works.

Sermon 25. Whenever you wish.

Listen carefully, dearest children and brothers. Receive the words of God with the ear of the body and with the ear of the heart, and fulfill them by living in holy ways.

The Lord knows that, after the loss of free will, no good can come from us by ourselves. Therefore he urges us not only to pray devoutly, but to pray persistently. He teaches us that whatever human nature lacks in its own power, it should seek through prayer from him, without whom nothing good can be done.

So let us come to him, as in the Gospel parable, in the night of tribulation. Let us humbly beg for the help of the one who foretold that his own people would suffer hardship in this world, so that, just as he conquered the world, he may make us conquer too.
And because the Almighty sometimes delays what we ask—not in order to lessen the reward, but to increase our merit—let us ask persistently that he give us the three loaves.

Bread is our most basic food. Here it signifies either the knowledge of the Holy Trinity, or the three principal virtues: faith, hope, and love, by which we make our way toward heaven. Let us ask for this persistently. And if the friend will not rise because of friendship, then at least because of our insistence he will rise and give what is needed.

God wants his petitioners to be persistent. His gifts are free, and they go beyond what we deserve. The ears of divine mercy are always open to our prayers, if we do not stop praying and do not waver in faith. For if an earthly father knows how to give good gifts to his children, how much more will the heavenly Father give his Spirit to those who ask him?
So let us confess our sins to one another, and pray for one another so that we may be saved. Constant prayer is very powerful. Prayer closed heaven, and prayer opened it again.

Since we celebrate the Rogation Days every year, I would like to recall where they began. Because of the people’s sins, a fierce plague of wolves was threatening them. The bishops of Gaul gathered at Vienne, and together they decreed that these days should be celebrated for three days. And when the Lord mercifully removed the plague, these days became an annual custom, to be celebrated throughout the provinces of Gaul before the Lord’s Ascension.
Let us also keep these days, as has been decreed in the councils, with the greatest reverence and devotion: with abstinence from meat and with humility of heart. We do this not merely to escape the fury of visible wolves, but so that we may overcome the temptations of invisible wolves—that is, unclean spirits.

On these days no one should dress in costly clothing. Our fathers decreed that we should mourn in sackcloth and ashes. Drunkenness and feasting must be forbidden. No one should presume to ride on horseback there. Let us all walk barefoot, and with contrition of heart let us implore God’s mercy for our sins—not only for our own sins, but for the needs of the whole Church.

Let us pray for all people: for the lord pope, for the king and those placed in high office, for the clergy, and for the whole Catholic people, so that we may live a quiet and peaceful life in all holiness.
