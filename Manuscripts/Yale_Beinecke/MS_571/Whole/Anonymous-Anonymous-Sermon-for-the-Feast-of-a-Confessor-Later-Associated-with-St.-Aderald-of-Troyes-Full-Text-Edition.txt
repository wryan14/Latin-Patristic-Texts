
by Anonymous
Patrologia Latina Volume 


Sermon for the Feast Day of One Confessor.

Dearly beloved brothers, you have come eagerly to celebrate the feast of this most holy confessor. So you must think very carefully about the intention and devotion with which you have gathered here. For even though all of you are celebrating the same day, not everyone has the same intention, and not everyone brings the same devotion.
Some people come so that, through the prayers of this great confessor, they may change the evil habits of their past lives—habits they are now ashamed to have lived in for so long. They want to pass from darkness into light, from falsehood into truth, from injustice into righteousness, and from vices into virtues. As the apostle Paul urges: “I appeal to you, by the mercy of God, to present your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy and pleasing to God; this is your reasonable service. And do not be conformed to this age, but be renewed in the freshness of your understanding, so that you may discern what the will of God is: what is good, pleasing, and perfect.” And this is God’s will: that by doing good you may silence the ignorance of foolish people.
Others suffer bodily sickness or weakness in their limbs. Some are blind, lame, mute, or deaf. Some are paralyzed or afflicted in mind. Some are tormented by the savage cruelty and rage of unclean spirits. These people gather for the splendid feast of this confessor so that, through his merits and intercession, they may obtain what they cannot obtain from living human beings, from medicines, or from any other human help. For he walked in the law of the Lord; he did not stand in the way of sinners; and he did not sit in the seat of corruption—that is, in the judgment of the wicked.
Meanwhile, some hurry to the holy chapels of the church with lights and various offerings, and together with others they beg for the mercy of God. Yet inside themselves they still have the devil as their possessor. He does not allow them to obey God’s commandments or to put an end to their sins. Instead, he burns more fiercely against them through each offense. He drives some toward greed, some toward pride, others toward lust and envy, others toward anger and murder, and many toward theft, sacrilege, and every kind of sinful vice. His aim is to make them eternally lost, condemned by God, and to drag with him, into everlasting and endless destruction, everyone he can.
Therefore, all of us who still have both the will and the ability, and who have come humbly to celebrate with deep devotion the solemn feast of this blessed confessor, must beware of the treachery of this most savage enemy. From the beginning of the world he has always been the enemy of the whole human race. Let us be careful that he does not corrupt us by any trick, deceive us by poisonous persuasion, or in any way tear from our hearts the root of the good intention that has brought us here. Beloved, let there be in us persevering faith, unshaken hope, and unfailing love. These bright virtues filled the soul of this blessed and glorious confessor. He was set on fire by the Holy Spirit, so that no worldly display, no glory of kings or princes, and no pleasant favor from relatives or friends could turn him away from faith in Christ, from hope in the blessed promise, or from love for God, who allowed himself to suffer for the redemption of the world.
Now he has been made a co-heir with Christ and dwells among the fiery choirs of angels. He is a companion of the apostles, a sharer with the martyrs, a colleague of the confessors, a friend of the virgins, and a fellow citizen of the patriarchs and prophets. He prays for sinners, that they may recover their senses and turn away from evil. He prays for the righteous, that they may persevere in good. And he pleads without ceasing for the well-being of the whole catholic Church. But we, dearly beloved brothers, are living amid the uncertain conditions of this world: sometimes times of peace, sometimes times of hardship. Since we have come to commemorate this solemn day, let us take the greatest care to walk in his footsteps, just as he followed the Lord. In him, let us venerate the grace of the Redeemer, who works miracles on earth to glorify his confessor and to bring him praise. And let us wait in hope to receive divine gifts from heaven—gifts that we obtain from God through this saint’s intercession and through the Lord himself.
There are many among us who do not know what these gifts are that heavenly mercy provides for us. And these gifts do not come because of our own merits, but because of the merits of those saints with whom this renowned confessor is joined, and with whom he intercedes for our needs. If someone does not know what these gifts are and asks about them, then every educated and wise person should answer, and every unlearned person should be told this: first, God’s grace is present in us because, when we did not yet exist, he powerfully created us; and when we were lost under the sentence of our own transgression, he mercifully redeemed us. Christ offered himself through the hands of the Jews to God the Father on the altar of the cross as a sacrifice of atonement for our sins—and not only for our sins, but also for the sins of the whole world. Then he granted us the spiritual grace of his baptism. In baptism, all sins are washed away, both original sins and actual sins, and the entrance into heavenly life is opened. After that comes the gift of the sevenfold Spirit through the laying on of bishops’ hands: to each person is given the spirit of wisdom and understanding, the spirit of counsel and strength, the spirit of knowledge and devotion, and the fear of the Lord.
Later, after all these gifts have been received but not preserved, God does not want the sinner to be persuaded into despair. So from the hidden depths of divine compassion came the reconciliation of penance. When the sinner receives it, he groans. He sheds tears of sorrow with sighs. He flees the softness of the flesh. He learns to love a harsher way of eating, drinking, and sleeping. He desires to endure the severity of heat and cold. He learns perseverance in vigils and prayers. He turns away from arrogance and loves humility. He despises quarrels and detests lawsuits and disputes. He embraces patience. He keeps watch over the pursuit of chastity. He tramples lust like dung. He avoids greed as an enemy, so that almsgiving may not fail. For he knows that, as Scripture says, the flesh desires against the spirit, and the spirit against the flesh, so that we do not simply do whatever we want.
Finally, in the Church of this present life, we are mixed together: some good, some evil. If we strive to follow in the footsteps of this saint, then we should seek from God not only spiritual gifts, but also bodily gifts—the things by which our bodies are sustained and through which we may reap heavenly rewards. Let us go to God and ask from him what we need, in a worthy and praiseworthy way. Let us do, together with this confessor, what our Lord Jesus Christ commands: “First seek the kingdom of God and his righteousness, and all these things will be added to you.” For what does it profit a person to gain the whole world and suffer the loss of his own soul? Or what will a person give in exchange for his soul? The answer is understood: nothing. So if we desire to become companions of this renowned confessor in the beauty of the heavenly paradise, let us renounce the works of the flesh: impurity, greed, lust, anger, quarrels, divisions, pride, envy, covetousness, and similar things. The apostle warned us beforehand that those who do such things will not obtain the kingdom of God. Let us take up the cross of Christ and follow him. Let us present our bodies as a living sacrifice, holy and pleasing to God. Let us crucify the old self—not living in feasting and drunkenness, not in sexual immorality and shamelessness, not in rivalry and jealousy—but let us put on the Lord Jesus Christ, who wants everyone to be saved and to come to the knowledge of the truth. Before his most merciful face, let us prostrate ourselves humbly and devoutly. Let us offer him countless praises and thanks. He has comforted us up to this very day amid so many disturbances of this age. He has mercifully rescued us from the swords of pagans and from the hands of persecutors. And although we sinned, he did not punish us immediately; rather, like the most loving Lord, he patiently endured us, crying out through the prophet: “I do not desire the death of the sinner, but that he should turn back and live.” Let us praise and bless him, for he has granted us the gift of this unexpected peace. Let us pray that, by the prayers of his saints, he will govern and protect us—his people, the sheep of his pasture, and the work of his hands. May he not allow us to be worn down by the war of vices, but rather allow us to enjoy the tranquility of virtues and take delight in it. Then, after the course of this passing peace, we may be able to arrive at that peace which is in heaven and remains without end. Let us offer ourselves to God, so that through works of love we may become his dwelling place. For whoever remains in love remains in God, and God remains in him. Let us love one another, because he first loved us. For if someone does not love the brother whom he sees, how can he love God whom he does not see? And we have this commandment from God: whoever loves God must also love his brother. For in Christ we are all brothers. Let us render to the Lord the vows we owe: offerings and sacrifices, tithes and firstfruits, praise and thanksgiving, purity of heart, purity of speech, and all the other things God requires from us. Let us not be greedy or lazy in faithfully paying these things to our Lord, because even our very existence is something we have received from him.
For he himself shaped our bodies in our mothers’ wombs, gave them life by the spirit, and brought us out into this light. And here, in this life, we cannot live at all without his gifts. He makes his sun rise on the good and the evil, and sends rain on the righteous and the unrighteous. Without his light, we cannot know the paths of his commandments. Without his gift, we gain neither health for our bodies nor salvation for our souls. Without his ordering of creation, the sky gives no rain, and the earth cannot bring forth the fruits by which all human beings, all livestock, and every living creature are sustained.
So let us fear, love, and honor this magnificent Creator and strict Judge. Let us pray to him with all our hearts, asking him to free us from the snare of eternal ruin—where there is endless weeping and groaning and the terror of darkness, where there is unquenchable fire, unbearable cold, and worms that do not die—and to lead us instead to the blessed and joyful places of heaven. There the light never fails, joy lasts forever, no sickness is felt, no hunger or thirst is known, no enemy is feared, and everyone beholds only the vision of God.
And because our conscience accuses us of our guilt, and because for that reason we judge ourselves unworthy to be heard, let us, during this solemn feast, tearfully ask for the prayers of this blessed confessor and most holy, illustrious bishop. Through his intercession, may we be helped in this present life by divine aid, and in the blessed life to come may we be found worthy to join the heavenly choirs of angels and of all the saints. May this be granted by the help of our Lord and God, Jesus Christ, who is always wonderful in his saints, now and through all ages of ages. Amen.
Here ends the sermon.
